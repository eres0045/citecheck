# Sample notes — SYNTHETIC (demo only, not Ronit's real notes)

## Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert sunlight,
water and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen. It takes place mainly
in the leaves, inside organelles called chloroplasts. Chlorophyll, the
green pigment in chloroplasts, absorbs light energy, mostly in the blue
and red parts of the spectrum.

The overall equation is: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2.
The light-dependent reactions take place on the thylakoid membranes and
produce ATP and NADPH. The Calvin cycle then uses ATP and NADPH to fix
carbon dioxide into glucose in the stroma.

## Cell respiration

Cellular respiration releases energy from glucose in the mitochondria.
The three main stages are glycolysis in the cytoplasm, the Krebs cycle
in the mitochondrial matrix, and the electron transport chain on the
inner mitochondrial membrane. The overall equation is the reverse of
photosynthesis: C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP.

A common exam mistake is to confuse the sites: photosynthesis happens
in the chloroplast, while respiration happens in the mitochondrion.

## Old syllabus note

In the 2019 syllabus, the Krebs cycle was listed as carrying 3 marks
in the board exam. The 2021 revision changed the marking scheme, so
any note quoting the 2019 marks should be checked against the current
syllabus before the exam.
